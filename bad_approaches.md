# Bad Approaches — What NOT to do in my LinkedIn posts

Anti-patterns derived from my own "A lot of problems" reference card and confirmed against my 123-post history.

---

## 1. Long, flat, emoji-less title

**Bad:** `Pleased to share a new preprint on arXiv today, "Universal quantum theory contains twisted logic"!`

**Why it fails:**
- LinkedIn previews show ~2 lines before "...see more". A long title eats both lines without earning the click.
- No emoji as the first character — nothing draws the eye while the reader scrolls.
- Generic "Pleased to share..." wording: corporate, low-energy, indistinguishable from every other academic post.

**Rule:** Title must be ONE short line, START with an emoji, and have the key noun in **bold unicode** (`𝗯𝗼𝗹𝗱`).

---

## 2. Acknowledgments at the top

Putting the list of co-authors / collaborators in the second paragraph wastes the reader's attention span.

**Why it fails:**
- Acks are useful for getting likes from tagged people — but only AFTER the reader is hooked.
- The reader doesn't care who the team is until they care about WHAT was done.
- Tag-heavy paragraphs look like noise during a scroll.

**Rule:** Acks go at the BOTTOM, after the body and CTA, opened with `🙏🏻 Thanks to …`.

---

## 3. Long, wall-of-text paragraphs

**Bad:** A 4–6 line dense paragraph explaining the result with no visual anchor and no whitespace.

**Why it fails:**
- LinkedIn's mobile width turns 4 lines of dense text into 10+ lines on a phone — a scroll-killer.
- No emoji at the start of each block = no visual landmark to re-orient after a glance.
- No `#hashtags` on highlighted concepts = the eye has nothing to lock onto.

**Rule:** Each idea = its own short paragraph (1–3 lines max), START with a meaningful emoji, blank line between paragraphs. Use `#hashtags` inline to highlight the 3–5 key concepts.

---

## 4. Links inside the post body

**Bad:** `Link: https://lnkd.in/xxxxx` placed mid-post.

**Why it fails:**
- The LinkedIn algorithm demotes posts that send the user OUT of the platform.
- A raw link in the middle is also a visual break that derails the read.

**Rule:** Move EVERY external link to the **first comment**. In the post itself, write `👇🏻 link in the first comment` or `👇🏻 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝘅𝗶𝘃 𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗸 in the first comment`.

---

## 5. Photos instead of GIFs

Static photos (especially "team in lab" shots, or worse: a photo with a cat / mascot on top) hurt scroll-stopping power.

**Why it fails:**
- A static image while scrolling reads as "ad, skip".
- Videos may not autoplay or take too long — the user keeps scrolling.
- A light GIF (a quick loop, a diagram animation) catches the eye and gets the reader to PAUSE without committing.

**Rule:** Prefer a **light GIF** over a photo or video. Photos only if there's genuinely no alternative.

---

## 6. Corporate / templated phrasing

**Bad starters:**
- `Pleased to share…`
- `I am excited to announce…`
- `Honored to be part of…`

**Why it fails:**
- These are LinkedIn-bingo phrases that signal "this is going to be a generic update".
- Authentic voice is what makes my posts recognizable; templated openers erase it.

**Rule:** Open with an emoji + a punchy, specific noun phrase. Examples that work for me:
- `🎊 𝗡𝗲𝘄 𝗽𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗿𝗶𝗻𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝗼𝘂𝘁!`
- `🔥 New job position!`
- `🤯 F*ck Agentic Coding`
- `🐘 L'elefante nella stanza della computazione quantistica`

---

## 7. No CTA / no question

A post that ends on a flat statement gives the reader nothing to do.

**Why it fails:**
- The LinkedIn algorithm rewards comments. No CTA = no comments = no reach.
- A question invites the reader into a conversation; a statement closes the door.

**Rule:** End with EITHER a question (`Are you curious? / Are you a quantum algorithm designer?`) OR a `👇🏻 first comment` pointer (ideally both). Across my history, ~45% of posts include a question and ~32% reference the first comment.

---

## 8. Missing emphasis (no bold / italic styling)

A paragraph that contains no visual emphasis treats every word as equally important.

**Why it fails:**
- Skim-readers (most of LinkedIn) can't grab the 2–3 key terms.
- The eye has nowhere to land.

**Rule:** Apply **bold unicode** (`𝗯𝗼𝗹𝗱`) to the 1–3 most important concepts per paragraph. Use **italic unicode** (`𝘪𝘵𝘢𝘭𝘪𝘤`) for asides, definitions, or "soft voice" parenthetical comments — like a whispered remark.

---

## 9. Generic hashtags spam at the bottom

**Bad:** `#quantum #computing #ai #ml #research #science #pisa #italy #technology #innovation`

**Why it fails:**
- A wall of 10+ hashtags at the bottom looks like SEO spam.
- My average is ~3.5 hashtags per post, and most are woven INLINE inside sentences (e.g., `…the #complexity of the quantum classifier`).

**Rule:** 2–5 hashtags max. Prefer INLINE on a highlighted noun. Reserve a small bottom cluster only for event/community tags (e.g., `#CodemotionCommunity #CodemotionSpeaker`).

---

## 10. Mixing too many ideas in one post

Trying to announce a paper + a talk + a podcast + a job opening in one post.

**Why it fails:**
- Each topic deserves its own attention budget.
- A multi-topic post is a multi-CTA post = zero CTA in practice.

**Rule:** One post = one topic = one CTA.
